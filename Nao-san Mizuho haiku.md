Mizuho haiku

Two gentle minds 

let seeds grow into light

the mountain smile again



Ah, that context changes the tone beautifully. Since this is a thank-you / commemorative haiku for two people from Mizuho Bank who discussed funding for the Mitsue project, an indirect reference to “Mizuho” is elegant and more poetic than naming the bank directly.

A useful nuance: 瑞穂 (Mizuho) literally means “auspicious rice ears / abundant harvest”, and 水 (mizu, water) naturally evokes nurturing, growth, and life. We can weave water, spring, or abundant grain imagery into the poem.

Here are several refined options.

### Version 1 — subtle “Mizuho” reference (my favorite)

English

Gentle water flows— seeds rise quietly to light; mountain smiles again.

Japanese

水の縁 光へ芽吹く種 山笑う

Furigana

水《みず》の縁《えにし》 光《ひかり》へ芽吹《めぶ》く種《たね》 山《やま》笑《わら》う

- 水の縁 (mizu no enishi) = “the bond of water,” a gentle allusion to Mizuho without naming it directly.
- 山笑う is the classical spring expression “the mountains smile.”

### Version 2 — slightly more explicit toward Mizuho

English

Waters of abundance— two hearts lift the hidden seeds; the mountain smiles.

Japanese

瑞の水 二人の心種育て 山笑う

Furigana

瑞《みず》の水《みず》 二人《ふたり》の心《こころ》種《たね》育《そだ》て 山《やま》笑《わら》う

Here 瑞 (mizu) hints at 瑞穂 (Mizuho) through the character meaning auspicious.

### Version 3 — includes the meeting and future feeling

English

After kind waters, seeds begin their climb to light— Mitsue smiles too.

Japanese

水の声 光へ伸びる種 御杖笑む

Furigana

水《みず》の声《こえ》 光《ひかり》へ伸《の》びる種《たね》 御杖《みつえ》笑《え》む

This one directly honors 御杖 (Mitsue).

### A polished presentation piece

For a message to the two Mizuho colleagues, I would choose this final form:

Edit

**水の縁**
**光へ芽吹く種**
**山笑う**

*みずのえにし*
*ひかりへめぶくたね*
*やまわらう*

**Bonded by gentle waters—**
**seeds quietly sprout toward the light;**
**the mountains smile again.**

It keeps the Mizuho reference elegant and indirect, expresses funding as nurturing growth, and ends with the hopeful image that the mountains—and by implication Mitsue village—smile once more.
